Can You Delete Fake Reviews on Trustpilot and How to Do It Legally

By RMA — Web Reputation Agency

Businesses operating in Italy frequently face the question of whether rimozione recensioni Trustpilot is possible when the content appears fabricated. Trustpilot maintains an open platform where anyone who claims a genuine experience can post feedback. At the same time the company invests heavily in automated detection systems and human moderation to protect review authenticity and consumer protection. Removal of content is therefore limited to cases that breach published guidelines or that meet the legal threshold for unlawful statements under Italian law.

This article describes the complete process used by Trustpilot to identify non-genuine material, the exact steps a company must follow to submit a report, the evidence required, typical processing times, and the options available when the platform declines to act. It also covers the Italian legal framework surrounding diffamazione and the practical measures that reduce the risk of future attacks. All information is drawn from Trustpilot’s official guidelines and established Italian legislation, including the Digital Services Act notice-and-action mechanism.

How Trustpilot Determines Fake Reviews

Trustpilot subjects every submitted review to automated screening before it becomes visible. The systems examine a wide range of behavioural and technical signals: account age, previous review history, device and IP consistency, linguistic patterns, timing of submission, and relationships between the reviewer and the reviewed company. When the software detects anomalies it either blocks the review immediately or routes it to the Content Integrity Team for manual assessment. In 2024 the platform reported that approximately 90 percent of detected fake reviews were removed by automated technology alone.

Platform moderation also relies on community reports. Both consumers and businesses can flag individual reviews. Businesses that have claimed a Trustpilot Business profile gain access to additional tools that allow them to attach supporting documentation and to request reference numbers linking a review to a specific transaction. The combination of machine learning and human review is designed to maintain a balance between openness and the need to prevent star rating manipulation or coordinated review bombing.

A review is treated as non-genuine when it does not reflect an actual experience with the business. This includes content written by individuals who never purchased a product or used a service, reviews posted by competitors or their associates, paid or incentivised posts, and reviews that clearly refer to a different company. Trustpilot’s guidelines explicitly prohibit these practices because they undermine the reliability of the TrustScore and mislead consumers.

Signs of a Fake Review

Several observable characteristics commonly indicate that a review may not be based on a real interaction. A newly created account that contains only one review and was opened shortly before the post is one of the most frequent indicators. Generic language that lacks any concrete detail about a product, service, date, location or staff member also raises suspicion. Clusters of similar one-star ratings appearing within a short time window, often sharing near-identical phrasing, suggest possible coordinated activity.

Additional signals include a reviewer profile that rates only companies in the same narrow sector and always assigns the lowest score, or a reviewer who fails to respond when the business requests further information through Trustpilot’s official tools. When the claimed purchase or service date does not appear in the company’s order system, CRM or support logs, the discrepancy becomes strong factual evidence. None of these signs alone guarantees removal, yet together they form the basis for a well-documented report.

What Trustpilot Considers a Violation

Trustpilot’s guidelines list clear categories that justify removal. Content that is not based on a genuine experience is the primary ground. Reviews containing harmful or illegal material, personal data that should not be public, advertising disguised as feedback, or statements about a completely different business also breach the rules. Incentivised reviews and posts written by individuals with an obvious conflict of interest (current employees, competitors) are likewise prohibited.

The platform does not remove a review simply because it is negative or because the business finds the language critical. Opinion, even when strongly expressed, remains protected provided it rests on a real experience. Only concrete guideline violations or demonstrably false statements of fact trigger action. Understanding this distinction is essential before any attempt at rimuovere recensioni false Trustpilot or eliminare recensioni false begins.

Official Way of Removal Through Trustpilot

The first and most direct route for any company seeking Trustpilot review removal is the platform’s own reporting system. Companies must claim and verify their business profile at business.trustpilot.com. Once the profile is active, the business can flag individual reviews from the dashboard. The process is free and does not require external intermediaries at the initial stage. Using the official channel ensures that the report is processed by the Content Integrity Team according to documented procedures.

Before submitting a flag the company should prepare a clear factual explanation that references the specific guideline breached and attaches supporting documentation. Emotional language or general claims that a review is “unfair” are routinely dismissed. Precise statements that link the reviewer’s claims to internal records produce faster and more reliable outcomes. After submission the review remains visible while the investigation proceeds, except in cases flagged as harmful or illegal, which may be temporarily hidden.

How to File a Complaint

The practical sequence is straightforward. Log into the Trustpilot Business account and open the Manage reviews section. Locate the service or product review in the Inbox. Click the flag icon beneath the review. Select the most accurate reason from the available options (not based on a genuine experience, harmful or illegal content, personal information, advertising or promotional content, conflict of interest). Depending on the reason chosen, the system may request additional details or ask the user to highlight specific problematic passages.

After selecting the reason, write a concise explanation that states the factual discrepancy and references any attached files. Confirm that the report is submitted in good faith and that misuse of the flagging tool is understood to be a breach of the guidelines. Submit the report. An automated confirmation is usually issued within 24–48 hours. The status of the flag can be monitored inside the Reporting Activity area of the dashboard. If Trustpilot contacts the reviewer for verification, the reviewer is typically given three days to respond.

What Evidence Is Needed

Strong documentation is the decisive factor in successful cases. The most persuasive materials are internal records that demonstrate the absence of any transaction matching the reviewer’s name, email, claimed order date or described experience. Screenshots of filtered order databases covering a relevant time window, CRM exports, support ticket logs and email correspondence with the company’s domain visible all carry weight. When the review contains specific factual claims that can be disproved (non-existent product, non-existent location, impossible date), documentary contradiction of those claims strengthens the report.

Additional useful evidence includes LinkedIn or public-profile screenshots showing that the reviewer is employed by a competitor, records of multiple similar reviews posted from the same IP range or device cluster, and proof that the reviewer was offered payment or incentives. All documents should be redacted to remove sensitive customer data while preserving the information needed for verification. Trustpilot accepts common file formats attached to the flag or supplied in subsequent correspondence.

How Long the Review Takes

Processing times vary according to complexity and current workload. Straightforward cases with clear documentary evidence are often decided within three to seven business days. When Trustpilot must contact the reviewer for additional verification or when the case involves coordinated patterns across multiple reviews, the timeline can extend to two or three weeks. The platform notifies the reporting business by email once a decision is reached. If the review is removed, the TrustScore is recalculated automatically. If the flag is rejected, the decision email usually contains a brief explanation and the option to reply with further information.

What to Do If Trustpilot Rejected the Complaint

A rejection does not end the process. Trustpilot allows businesses to reply to the decision email and supply additional evidence or clarify points that may have been overlooked. The Content Integrity Team then reassesses the case manually. Providing new documentation that was not available at the time of the original flag, or highlighting previously unnoticed inconsistencies in the reviewer’s claims, can reverse an initial decision.

If the second review still results in rejection, the company may escalate through Trustpilot’s formal legal channel for content alleged to be unlawful. This channel is distinct from ordinary guideline flagging and is intended for statements that meet the threshold of illegal content under applicable law. Parallel to the platform process, the business can also explore the legal instruments available under Italian law, which are examined in the following section. Throughout this stage it remains important to keep complete records of every communication, screenshot and document exchanged, as these materials may later support a formal notice or court application.

Legal Ways of Influence

When platform procedures do not produce removal, Italian law offers additional avenues. The Digital Services Act imposes notice-and-action obligations on hosting platforms, including Trustpilot. A properly drafted notice that identifies the exact URL, qualifies the content as unlawful, and attaches supporting evidence can trigger the platform’s duty to assess and, where appropriate, remove the material. Italian courts have clarified that knowledge of illegality can arise from any credible communication, not only from a formal court order.

Companies based in Italy or targeting Italian consumers can also rely on national rules concerning online statements. The most relevant instrument is the criminal and civil framework governing diffamazione. Parallel civil actions for damages and, in urgent cases, interim measures under article 700 of the Code of Civil Procedure may be considered when the harm is serious and ongoing. Each of these routes requires careful preparation and, in most instances, professional legal assistance.

Diffamazione

Under article 595 of the Italian Criminal Code, diffamazione consists of offending another person’s reputation by communicating with more than one person. When the communication occurs through the internet the offence is aggravated. A review that asserts specific false facts capable of damaging a company’s commercial reputation can therefore fall within the scope of the provision. The three-month limitation period for filing a criminal complaint begins from the moment the injured party becomes aware of the content.

A successful complaint requires demonstration that the statements are false, that they were communicated to third parties, and that they caused reputational harm. Opinion, value judgements and accurate descriptions of real experiences remain protected by the right of criticism. Only verifiable falsehoods presented as fact support a claim. Once a complaint is filed, the public prosecutor may open an investigation. In parallel, a civil action can seek both removal of the content and compensation for moral and economic damage. Formal notices (diffida) sent to the reviewer and to the platform often precede formal proceedings and can produce voluntary deletion.

Contacting the Author of the Review

When the reviewer is identifiable, a measured private approach is sometimes effective. A concise message that points out the factual inaccuracies, attaches supporting evidence and politely requests correction or deletion can lead to voluntary removal. The communication should avoid threats or aggressive language, as these may escalate the situation. If the reviewer does not respond or refuses to act, the same message and evidence can later form part of a formal legal notice.

Where the account is anonymous, identification may require a court order directing Trustpilot to disclose the underlying registration data. Such requests are evaluated under data-protection rules and the proportionality principles of Italian civil procedure. Only when the statements meet the legal threshold for unlawful content is disclosure ordinarily granted. Businesses should therefore assess the strength of the case with qualified counsel before initiating identification proceedings.

Prevention of Fake Reviews on Trustpilot

While complete elimination of fabricated content is impossible, several practical measures substantially reduce exposure. Claiming the Trustpilot Business profile gives the company control over responses, access to flagging tools and visibility of invitation statistics. Consistent use of Trustpilot’s official automatic invitation system generates a steady flow of verified reviews that dilutes the impact of any isolated fake entry. Invitations must be sent fairly to all customers who have had a genuine experience, without selection based on expected sentiment and without any form of incentive.

Monitoring the profile regularly allows early detection of sudden clusters. When a suspicious pattern appears, prompt flagging with supporting evidence improves the chance of rapid removal. Public replies to genuine negative reviews demonstrate responsiveness and can limit the reputational effect of the comment. Maintaining clear internal records of every transaction and customer interaction ensures that evidence is readily available if a non-genuine review later appears.

Additional safeguards include training staff never to solicit or purchase reviews, documenting any approaches by third parties offering paid ratings, and using Trustpilot’s whistleblower channel to report organised review sellers. Over time a robust volume of authentic, invitation-only reviews creates a TrustScore that is statistically more resilient to isolated attacks. Companies that treat gestione Trustpilot as an ongoing operational process rather than a reactive task experience fewer disruptions and maintain higher consumer trust.

  • Claim and verify the business profile immediately after the company begins receiving reviews.
  • Configure automatic, neutral invitations triggered after delivery or service completion.
  • Keep searchable transaction logs covering at least the previous twelve months.
  • Respond publicly and factually to every genuine negative review within a short timeframe.
  • Flag suspicious content promptly and attach documentary evidence rather than general complaints.

These steps form the foundation of effective Trustpilot reputation management and reduce reliance on later corrective action.

Conclusion

Legitimate rimozione recensioni false is possible when the content breaches Trustpilot guidelines or meets the legal definition of unlawful statements under Italian law. When that process is exhausted, the Digital Services Act notice-and-action mechanism and the Italian framework on diffamazione provide further avenues. Prevention through verified invitation systems, consistent monitoring and professional responses remains the most efficient long-term strategy.
