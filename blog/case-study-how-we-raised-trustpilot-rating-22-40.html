Case Study: How We Raised a Trustpilot Rating from 2.2 to 4.0

By RMA — Web Reputation Agency

RMA — Reputation Management Agency (webreputation.agency) provides specialised Trustpilot reputation management and servizi gestione reputazione for companies based in Italy. This case study records the full sequence of actions that moved one client’s Trustpilot score from 2.2 to 4.0 over twelve months. The work combined forensic review analysis, structured rimozione recensioni Trustpilot where policy allowed, systematic gestione recensioni negative, controlled review generation and continuous monitoraggio recensioni. Every tactic remained inside Trustpilot’s published rules and Italian consumer law. The outcome shows how professional online review management and reputation repair can rebuild platform reputation, strengthen social proof and protect conversion rate reviews without artificial inflation.

Starting Situation of the Client

The client operated a mid-sized Italian e-commerce business focused on consumer electronics and small domestic appliances. When cooperation began, the public Trustpilot profile displayed a rating medio of 2.2 stars calculated from roughly 180 reviews. Approximately two-thirds of the visible feedback carried one or two stars. The remaining comments formed a thin layer of three- to five-star entries that could not counterbalance the volume of critical content. The profile had received almost no structured attention for eighteen months. Response rate to customer messages stayed below 10 %. Invitation to review messages were sent sporadically and without any buyer segmentation. No internal protocol existed for identifying service failures before they appeared as public complaints.

Website analytics showed that a noticeable share of visitors who clicked through from the Trustpilot page left the checkout process. Support tickets regularly cited the low star rating as a reason for hesitation. Sales staff reported that prospects requested additional proof of reliability because the visible platform reputation looked unreliable. Earlier attempts at replying to negative comments had been irregular, defensive in tone and often posted weeks after the original review. No organised programme for review generation was active, so the ratio of positive to negative feedback continued to decline.

On the technical side the Trustpilot business account used only basic email notifications. There was no live dashboard for response times, no alert system for sudden clusters of one-star reviews and no connection to the internal CRM that would let customer-service agents resolve issues offline before a complaint became public. The invitation system relied on a single generic template that produced low open and completion rates. Consequently the number of recensioni verificate remained low, which further reduced the perceived authenticity of the few positive comments present.

What Problems Were Present

The dominant issue was a concentration of negative reviews describing late deliveries, incomplete shipments and slow after-sales handling. Several entries claimed that refund requests had been ignored. A smaller group questioned product authenticity. Because the company had not answered most of these posts, the claims appeared unchallenged to later readers. The low response rate itself became a visible metric on the Trustpilot profile and reinforced the impression of indifference.

A second structural weakness was the imbalance between verified and unverified feedback. Most one-star reviews came from actual purchasers, yet the company rarely invited recent satisfied buyers to leave feedback. As a result the Trustpilot score drifted downward even after operational improvements had been introduced inside the warehouse and support teams. Trustpilot’s ranking logic gives heavier weight to recent activity; without a steady stream of new positive reviews the older negative content continued to control the average.

Internal records showed that customer-service agents already knew about many of the complaints but had no clear escalation route to a reputation team. The same operational bottlenecks therefore reappeared in successive reviews. In addition there was no process for requesting removal of content that broke Trustpilot’s guidelines—reviews containing personal insults, invented order numbers or posts from people who had never been customers. These non-compliant items remained visible and kept the rating medio suppressed.

Business Consequences of a Low Rating

The commercial effects were measurable. Conversion tracking linked to Trustpilot referrals indicated an approximate 18 % decline compared with the previous year. Average order value among visitors arriving from the Trustpilot profile sat below the site-wide average, pointing to reduced trust or heightened price sensitivity. Paid-search campaigns that once converted efficiently began to show higher acquisition costs once the low star rating appeared in ad extensions and knowledge panels.

New wholesale discussions slowed because many B2B buyers check public review platforms before opening commercial talks. Existing partners occasionally referred to the Trustpilot score during contract renewals and used it as leverage for stricter payment terms. Customer-lifetime-value calculations revealed that buyers who left negative reviews were less likely to place a second order, and the public negative content discouraged others from becoming repeat customers. Over time the unmanaged profile also increased the risk of copycat complaints: once unanswered critical reviews become common, later customers feel freer to post extreme language because they expect no factual reply.

Strategy We Applied

The method chosen for Trustpilot reputation management followed a four-stage sequence built for lasting improvement rather than short-term score jumps. Each stage rested on the results of the previous one and was measured against concrete operational indicators: reduction of policy-violating content, rise in response rate, growth in verified positive reviews and stabilisation of the star rating. All activity stayed inside Trustpilot’s rules; no reviews were purchased, no prohibited incentives were offered and no automated posting tools were used.

The central aim was to restore a balanced public record in which authentic positive feedback and transparent handling of legitimate criticism could coexist. Every review—positive or negative—was treated as data that could improve both the external profile and the client’s internal processes. Parallel work streams addressed content eligible for removal, content that required a public reply, and the controlled generation of new verified reviews from recent buyers. This combination of rimozione recensioni Trustpilot, gestione recensioni negative and structured review generation formed the practical core of the reputation repair programme.

Stage 1. Analysis and Cleaning

Work opened with a complete audit of the existing review set. Each entry was examined for content type, compliance with Trustpilot policies, underlying operational cause and potential for constructive dialogue. Reviews that contained personal attacks, fabricated order references or claims that could not be linked to an actual purchase were marked for formal removal requests. Documented evidence packages were prepared for Trustpilot’s moderation team, including order histories, shipping records and correspondence logs where available.

The audit also mapped the chronological distribution of negative reviews to identify periods of operational stress—peak sales seasons, warehouse moves or changes in carrier contracts. These time clusters later guided internal process reviews. Simultaneously a simple tagging system was introduced inside the client’s CRM so that every future support ticket that risked becoming a public review received an internal flag and a fixed resolution deadline.

Cleaning focused exclusively on content that violated Trustpilot’s published guidelines. Legitimate critical reviews were left in place; the goal was accuracy, not erasure. Once non-compliant items were removed or hidden by Trustpilot, the visible rating medio rose modestly and the remaining negative reviews became easier to address because they reflected real service issues rather than policy breaches. This first stage created a cleaner baseline on which later improvements could be measured.

Practical Steps Inside Stage 1

  • Export of the full review history and classification by star rating, date, verification status and content category.
  • Preparation of evidence files for every removal request, limited to policy violations only.
  • Installation of internal alerts that notify the reputation team whenever a new one- or two-star review appears.
  • Creation of a shared log that records the outcome of each removal request and the time taken by Trustpilot to respond.

Stage 2. Work with Existing Reviews

After the profile had been cleaned of non-compliant content, attention shifted to the remaining reviews. Every open negative review received a factual, calm public reply within a defined time window. Replies acknowledged the customer’s experience, stated what had been checked internally and, where appropriate, offered a concrete next step such as a replacement shipment or a refund confirmation. The tone remained neutral and professional; defensive language and generic apologies were avoided.

Positive and neutral reviews were also answered, though more briefly, in order to raise the overall response rate visible on the Trustpilot profile. A higher response rate signals active management and improves the perceived reliability of the business. Templates were prepared for the most common situations (delivery delay, damaged item, missing accessory) so that replies stayed consistent while still allowing personalisation with order-specific details.

Where a review described a problem that had already been solved offline, the public reply simply confirmed the resolution and invited the customer to update the original review if they wished. Trustpilot does not require customers to change their rating, yet a factual update from the buyer often softens the impact of the original low score. This stage of gestione recensioni negative and customer review management therefore served two purposes: it demonstrated responsiveness to future readers and it gave past customers a clear path to revise their feedback when the underlying issue had been fixed.

Stage 3. Generation of Positive Reviews

With the existing profile stabilised, the next priority was to increase the flow of authentic positive feedback. A segmented invitation-to-review programme was designed. Buyers who had received their order, confirmed delivery and raised no support ticket within a set number of days received a single, clearly worded invitation. The message explained that feedback helps other customers and contained a direct link to the company’s Trustpilot review form. No discounts, gifts or other incentives prohibited by Trustpilot were offered.

Invitation timing was adjusted according to product category: faster-moving items received the request earlier, while higher-value products were given a longer usage window before the invitation was sent. Open and completion rates were tracked weekly so that subject lines and send times could be refined. The same process also captured neutral and critical feedback; every review was treated as useful data. Over successive months the volume of recensioni verificate rose steadily, shifting the balance of the star rating upward without any artificial acceleration.

Internal reporting linked each new review back to the operational team responsible for the order. Positive reviews were used as recognition; critical ones triggered process checks. This closed-loop approach turned review generation into a continuous improvement tool rather than a pure marketing exercise. The combination of careful invitation design and operational follow-up produced a sustainable increase in authentic five-star feedback and supported the broader goal of come migliorare Trustpilot scores through genuine customer experience.

Stage 4. Monitoring and Protection

Once the rating had begun to climb, a permanent monitoring layer was installed. Daily checks captured new reviews, response times and any sudden changes in volume or sentiment. Weekly reports summarised the Trustpilot score movement, response rate and the ratio of positive to negative feedback. Monthly reviews examined longer trends and compared the public rating with internal customer-satisfaction metrics.

Protection measures included rapid escalation of any new policy-violating content, continued use of the structured reply templates and periodic refresher training for support staff on how operational issues later appear as public reviews. The monitoring system also watched for coordinated or unusual review patterns that might indicate attempts to manipulate the profile. Because the account was now actively managed, such attempts could be reported to Trustpilot promptly.

This final stage of online review management and business review management ensured that the gains achieved in the first three stages did not erode. Continuous monitoraggio recensioni kept the platform reputation stable and provided early warning of new operational risks. The same framework remains in place today and forms the core of the ongoing servizi gestione reputazione delivered to the client.

Results After 3 / 6 / 12 Months

At the three-month mark the most visible change was the removal of non-compliant reviews and the rise in response rate above 80 %. The rating medio had moved from 2.2 to approximately 2.9. New verified positive reviews had begun to appear, although their volume was still modest. Internal process adjustments triggered by the early review analysis had already reduced the number of delivery-related complaints reaching the public profile.

By six months the star rating stood at 3.5. The invitation-to-review programme was producing a steady weekly flow of authentic feedback. Response time to new reviews averaged under 48 hours. Conversion rate reviews tracked from Trustpilot traffic had recovered roughly half of the earlier loss. Wholesale partners who had previously expressed concern noted the improved platform reputation during commercial discussions.

At twelve months the Trustpilot score reached 4.0. The profile contained a balanced mix of recent verified reviews, the response rate remained consistently high and the volume of one-star content had fallen sharply relative to total reviews. Social proof on the Trustpilot page now supported rather than undermined purchasing decisions. The same monitoring and reply protocols continue to protect the rating, demonstrating that structured Trustpilot reputation management and reputation repair can deliver durable results when applied consistently.

What Worked Best

Three elements produced the largest measurable impact. First, the disciplined removal of only those reviews that clearly broke Trustpilot rules cleaned the baseline without creating the appearance of suppression. Second, the combination of fast, factual replies to negative reviews and systematic invitation of recent buyers restored a healthy flow of authentic feedback. Third, the closed loop between public reviews and internal operations turned every piece of feedback into process improvement, reducing the future supply of legitimate complaints.

The decision to avoid any form of incentivised or purchased reviews proved essential. Trustpilot’s detection systems and Italian regulatory expectations both penalise artificial activity. By staying inside the rules the improved rating remained stable and credible. Equally important was the decision to answer every review, positive or negative; the visible response rate itself became a signal of active customer review management and contributed to the overall perception of reliability.

  • Policy-compliant cleaning of the existing profile created a trustworthy starting point.
  • Consistent public replies raised response rate and demonstrated accountability.
  • Segmented, incentive-free invitation programmes steadily increased the volume of recensioni verificate.

Typical Mistakes Companies Make with a Low Trustpilot Rating

Many businesses confronted with a low Trustpilot score attempt quick fixes that later create additional problems. One common error is to ignore negative reviews in the hope that they will become less visible over time. Because Trustpilot weights recent activity, silence allows older critical content to continue dominating the average. Another frequent mistake is to post defensive or generic replies that fail to address the specific complaint; such responses often provoke further negative comments from the original reviewer and from later readers.

Some companies try to compensate by sending aggressive invitation campaigns that include prohibited incentives. Trustpilot regularly removes reviews obtained this way and may flag the business account. Others focus exclusively on generating new five-star reviews while leaving operational root causes untouched; the result is a temporary rise followed by a fresh wave of legitimate complaints. A further recurring error is the absence of any monitoring system, so that a sudden cluster of negative reviews goes unnoticed until the rating medio has already dropped.

Finally, many organisations treat Trustpilot as a purely external marketing problem rather than a mirror of internal service quality. Without linking public feedback to warehouse, logistics and support processes, the same issues reappear indefinitely. Effective improve online reputation and restore online reputation work therefore requires both external review management and internal process discipline.

Conclusion

Moving a Trustpilot rating from 2.2 to 4.0 is achievable when the work is structured, compliant and sustained. The sequence applied in this case—thorough analysis and cleaning, systematic handling of existing reviews, controlled generation of authentic new feedback and continuous monitoring—produced a durable improvement in platform reputation and social proof. The same principles of gestione Trustpilot, online review management and reputation repair remain available to other Italian businesses that need to riparare reputazione online and strengthen their public standing.
