How to remove a defamatory Google review (and what to do when you can't)

By RMA — Web Reputation Agency

A single one-star review can quietly cost you customers for years. Most stay online for one reason: they were reported the wrong way, once, and then given up on.

Google does remove reviews — but only when the request matches one of its policies and is backed by the right evidence. Here is the order that works, from cheapest to most serious.

1. Confirm it actually breaks a policy

Google won't remove a review just because it's negative or unfair. It removes reviews that violate its content rules: fake reviews from people who were never customers, spam, conflicts of interest, hate speech, personal attacks, or content that's off-topic. A calm "the food was cold" stays. "The owner is a fraud who steals money" is a personal accusation you can act on.

Read the review closely and write down which specific rule it breaks. That single sentence is what every later step hangs on.

2. Flag it through the business profile

From your Google Business Profile, report the review and select the reason that matches the policy you identified. This is free and sometimes enough. It is also slow and often ignored on the first pass — so treat it as the opening move, not the whole game.

3. Escalate to Google support with evidence

If flagging goes nowhere, open a support case and make it easy to say yes. Attach proof: that the reviewer was never a client, that the claim is false, screenshots, dates, order numbers. Reference the exact policy by name. A reviewer who left the same attack on five unrelated businesses is a pattern worth documenting — it's often the fastest path to removal.

The request that gets actioned isn't the angriest one. It's the one that hands Google a clear policy violation and the evidence to prove it.

4. Use the legal route when the claim is false

When a review states something untrue as fact and damages you, this becomes a legal matter, not a support ticket. A formal notice to the reviewer or a court-backed request to the platform carries far more weight than a flag. This is where most people stall on their own — and where specialised help pays for itself.

When it genuinely can't be removed

Some reviews are negative, honest opinion. Those won't come down, and chasing them wastes energy. The move then isn't removal — it's dilution: steadily earning recent, genuine positive reviews so the old one sinks down the page and stops defining you. One bad review among forty recent good ones barely registers.

Reputation is rarely one dramatic fix. It's the right step, in the right order, repeated until the search result matches who you actually are.
